Brand exploration · The Medicare Workshop

Four logo concepts, drawn from the world your customers actually live in.

A logo for seniors shouldn't feel like a tech startup or an insurance conglomerate. It should feel like something they already trust. We studied three visual traditions to find marks that carry warmth, guidance, and hometown credibility.

What we studied

Porch-light and lantern vernacular (welcome, guidance home), Kentucky horse-country iconography (fence rails, rolling hills, sunrise), and mid-century schoolhouse & extension-service seals (trusted, civic, unhurried).

What we avoided

Caduceus/medical crosses (feels clinical), shields and eagles (feels governmental — also a compliance risk), swooshes and gradients (feels like every Medicare Advantage TV ad they already distrust).

The test for every mark

Would it look right on a church-bulletin ad, embroidered on a polo, and as a tiny Facebook profile picture? Seniors meet this brand in all three places.

Concept 01

The Porch Lantern

The idea: in every small town, a lit porch lantern means the same thing — someone's home, and you're welcome here. It's guidance and hospitality in one mark, and it quietly promises "we'll leave the light on while you figure this out." No medical imagery at all — which is exactly the point.

Palette

Bluegrass
Lantern glow
Oat paper
Evening green

Why it works

Reads instantly at favicon size (the glowing dot survives any scale). Warm without being soft. Zero governmental or clinical baggage.

Where it shines: embroidered polos, yard signs, the webinar waiting-room screen ("we'll leave the light on" is a ready-made tagline and email sign-off).
Concept 02

The Fence Rail Sunrise

The idea: the most Kentucky image there is — a sun coming up behind a horse-farm plank fence. Retirement framed as a morning, not a sunset. The fence rails double as an abstract "path forward," and the mark scales beautifully from billboard to button.

Palette

Plank green
First sun
Morning sky
Red barn

Why it works

Unmistakably local — Lexington people feel ownership of this image. Optimistic framing ("morning") counters the dread Medicare marketing usually trades on. Easy to adapt per market (different regional silhouette behind the same sun).

Where it shines: Facebook ad thumbnails (the sun disc pops at small sizes), market expansion (swap the fence for another region's silhouette while keeping the sun — a built-in brand system).
Concept 03

The Rocking Chair Check

The idea: a checkmark that doubles as a rocking chair on its rocker. It says the two things every attendee wants to feel in one gesture: "it's handled" (the check) and "now I can relax" (the chair). This is the cleverest of the four — the mark people will point at and smile once they see it.

Palette

Deep green
Oak rocker
Porch white
Walnut ink

Why it works

A checkmark is the universal symbol of "done right" — trust at a glance. The rocker arc adds the emotional payoff. Two strokes total: survives any print quality, any size, one-color church bulletins included.

Where it shines: the take-home checklist (the logo IS a checkmark — the brand and the deliverable rhyme), stamps/seals on the cheat sheet, animated intro for the webinar (check draws itself, then rocks once).
Concept 04

The Kitchen Table

The idea: where do real Medicare conversations actually happen? Not in an office — at the kitchen table, over coffee. Two cups facing each other under one roofline: hospitality, conversation between equals, and "come sit down, let's figure this out together." The roofline quietly makes it a home.

Palette

Table green
Coffee
Linen
Gingham

Why it works

The most emotionally literal of the four — hospitality you can feel. Two cups = two hosts, and also = "you and us." Naturally extends to photography direction: every brand photo is shot at a real kitchen table.

Where it shines: the About page story, direct mail (looks like an invitation, not an ad), and it hands you the webinar's opening line: "Pull up a chair."

Our recommendation

Lead with Concept 3 (Rocking Chair Check), with Concept 1 (Porch Lantern) as the close second.

The Rocking Chair Check is the rare mark that carries the whole brand promise — "it's handled, relax" — in two strokes, works in one color at any size, and rhymes with your actual deliverable (a checklist). It's ownable: nobody in the Medicare space has anything like it, and it will never be mistaken for a government agency or a TV-ad insurance brand.

The Porch Lantern wins if you want the warmest possible brand voice — "we'll leave the light on" is a tagline, an email signature, and a company value in six words. The Fence Rail Sunrise is the strongest choice if multi-market expansion is the priority, since the sun-plus-local-silhouette system scales city by city.

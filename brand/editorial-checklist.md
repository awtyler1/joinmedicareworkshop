# Editorial Checklist — The Medicare Workshop

Nothing publishes without passing every line. Articles, workbooks, landing
pages, emails, social posts: same gate. We are not doing this for ourselves.
We are doing this to serve seniors.

## The gate: True · Useful · Clear

**True**
- [ ] Every fact checked against a primary source (Medicare.gov, CMS, SSA)
- [ ] Sources linked at the bottom of the piece
- [ ] "Last verified: [Month Year]" shown near the title
- [ ] Year-sensitive numbers (premiums, deductibles, caps) match the current year
- [ ] Nothing crosses the educational line (no plan pitches, no "enroll" language)

**Useful and actionable**
- [ ] Answers a question a real person actually asked (from workshop Q&A,
      a phone call, or a comment; note which)
- [ ] The reader can act within five minutes of finishing: a date to check,
      a number to call, a worksheet line to fill in
- [ ] Ends with "Your next step," singular, with phone numbers and dates included
- [ ] Honest scope note included: who this does NOT apply to

**Clear**
- [ ] The first two sentences answer the question; context comes after
- [ ] Read aloud once; every sentence that stumbles was rewritten
- [ ] One idea per sentence, one job per piece
- [ ] No jargon a smart 78-year-old wouldn't use at their kitchen table
- [ ] Body text 20px or larger; headings from the token scale

## The five editing questions (ask in order)

1. What do people need right now?
2. How is this incredibly useful? (If "it's fine," it doesn't ship yet.)
3. How can it be more actionable?
4. How can it be simpler and easier to understand?
5. What would we cut? (High signal, little noise. Cut anything that only
   makes us sound good.)

## House style

- Periods and commas do the work. Em dash: at most one per page, usually zero.
- Short sentences. Active voice. "You" more than "we."
- One wink per page, maximum.
- Numbers are specific ("$185/month in 2026") never vague ("costs can vary").
- Every piece signed by a real person: Austin or Andrew, with license number.

## Where topics come from (in priority order)

1. Questions asked live in workshop Q&As (write them down every session)
2. Questions from phone calls and consults
3. Comments and DMs on the ads
4. Only then: search/keyword research to confirm phrasing, never to pick topics

## Sign-off

- [ ] Passed all boxes above
- [ ] Second person read it (Austin writes, Andrew gates, or reverse)
- [ ] Compliance footer present and verbatim

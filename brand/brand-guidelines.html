The Medicare Workshop
Brand Book

How we look, how we sound, and why. One rule above all the others: if a neighbor wouldn't say it, and grandma couldn't read it, it doesn't ship.

01 · Foundation

What the brand promises

We turn the most confusing hour of retirement into the clearest one: free, in plain English, with nothing for sale at the door.

Personality

A trusted neighbor who happens to be an expert. Warm first, sharp second. Confident enough to joke ("there are no pennies"), humble enough to never talk down.

We are

Plain-spoken · local · patient · radically honest about how we get paid · generous by default.

We are never

Clinical · governmental · fear-mongering · urgent for fake reasons · salesy · cute at the expense of clarity.

03 · Color

The palette: a Kentucky porch at golden hour

Every color is named for where it comes from, so decisions stay grounded. Bluegrass leads, Oak warms, Barn urges (sparingly), Linen grounds, Walnut speaks.

Bluegrass#1F4A38 · primary, CTAs, headings-on-dark
Oak#B97F24 · the rocker, accents, highlights
Barn#A34A2E · urgency & seals only. Never pure red.
Linen#F8F4EA · page ground
Walnut#2A2519 · text ink. Never #000.

Ratio rule

Roughly 70% Linen/Porch grounds, 20% Bluegrass/Evening, 8% Oak, 2% Barn. If a page feels "loud," the Oak or Barn share has crept up.

Contrast standard

All body text pairs meet WCAG AAA (Walnut on Linen ≈ 12:1). Minimum anywhere, including labels on Oak: 4.5:1. Test before shipping.

Semantic tokens

Components never reference raw hex. They use the role tokens in brand/tokens.css (--accent, --bg-card…), so a palette tune-up is a one-file change.

05 · Voice & Tone

Sound like a neighbor. Be as sharp as a closer.

The Hormozi structure stays: value stacks, the disarm, the guarantee. The sound is a front porch. Six laws:

1 · Grandma test

Every sentence must survive being read aloud to a smart 78-year-old. "Utilize" dies. "Optimize your coverage journey" dies twice.

2 · Honesty as the hook

Our best marketing line is the true one: how we get paid, said plainly. When in doubt, disclose more, sooner, more cheerfully.

3 · Real urgency only

Medicare's deadlines are genuinely scary. We never need fake countdown timers. Name the real date, explain the real penalty, stop.

4 · One wink per page

"There are no pennies" works because it's alone. Two jokes make us clever; one makes us trustworthy.

5 · Periods do the work

Short sentences, one idea each. Commas, colons, and periods carry the rhythm. Em dashes are a spice: at most one per page, and most pages need zero.

6 · Signal over noise

Every sentence must help the reader know, decide, or do something. If it only makes us sound good, cut it. High signal, little noise.

The full writing playbook, built from a study of the great explainers, lives at brand/writing-style.html: The Kitchen Table Voice. The repertoire of article forms that keeps our range wide while the voice stays constant is at brand/article-forms.html.

✕ Not this

"Act NOW!! Spots are almost GONE!"

"Navigating the Medicare landscape can be overwhelming."

"We're passionate about empowering seniors."

✓ This

"We cap each webinar so the Q&A stays personal. When a session fills, it's full."

"Medicare isn't hard because you can't understand it. It's hard because everyone explaining it is selling something."

"You never pay us a dime, and your price is the same with or without us."

07 · Photography

Kitchen tables, not stock handshakes

Setting

Real Kentucky places: kitchen tables, front porches, the actual office. Natural window light, golden-hour warmth. Never: gradient studio backdrops, skyline b-roll.

People

Austin and Andrew, actual attendees (with signed permission), real seniors mid-conversation: laughing, pointing at a page, holding coffee. Never: stock "confused senior staring at laptop," stock handshakes, lab coats.

Treatment

True-to-life color, slightly warm. No filters, no duotones, and never AI-generated people. If we wouldn't frame it on the office wall, it doesn't go on the site.

08 · Motion & Accessibility

Calm is a feature

Motion budget

One speed (180ms), one easing, hover states and the single logo animation. No parallax, no scroll-jacking, no carousels, nothing autoplays. prefers-reduced-motion is honored globally in tokens.css.

Non-negotiables

WCAG AA minimum (AAA for body text) · 20px body floor · 56px tap targets · visible focus rings (Oak, 3px) · every page operable by keyboard · phone number reachable within one screen at all times · forms never more than 4 fields.

10 · The Publishing Standard

We never publish slop

This site will grow into a library for seniors: articles, workbooks, checklists. Our reputation will be exactly the quality of that work. So every piece passes one gate before it ships, with no exceptions for deadlines.

True

Every fact verified against a primary source (Medicare.gov, CMS, SSA) and dated. Numbers change yearly; a stale premium figure is a broken promise. Each article states when it was last verified.

Useful & actionable

The reader can do something within five minutes of finishing: check a date, make a call, fill in a worksheet line. If a piece only informs, it isn't done. Add the action.

Clear

Grandma test, applied in writing: read it aloud, cut every sentence that stumbles. One idea per sentence. One job per piece. If it needs a glossary, simplify until it doesn't.

The lens is the reader, never us

We don't publish to look busy or to rank for keywords. We publish because a real person asked a real question. Editing happens through five questions, asked in order:

AskWhat it catches
What do people need right now?Topic selection. Sources: questions from workshop Q&As, phone calls, and Facebook comments. Real demand, not guesses.
How is this incredibly useful?Thin content. If the honest answer is "it's fine," it doesn't ship until it's the best answer to that question on the internet.
How can it be more actionable?Missing next steps. Every piece ends with exactly what to do, in order, with the phone numbers and dates filled in.
How can it be simpler?Jargon, hedging, throat-clearing intros. The first sentence answers the question. Context comes after, for those who want it.
What would we cut?Noise. High signal means the reader never skims to find the point. If a paragraph exists to make us sound good, it goes.

How we prime the reader to expect quality

Trust is set in the first five seconds, so every published piece carries the same signals: the answer up top in the first two sentences, a "last verified" date, a named author (Austin or Andrew, with license number), primary sources linked at the bottom, an honest "who this doesn't apply to" note, and one clear next action. Readers learn quickly that our pages respect their time. That expectation is the brand.

The working checklist lives at brand/editorial-checklist.md. Nothing publishes without passing it.

11 · How to use this system

Building a new page in three moves

1. Import brand/tokens.css. 2. Assemble from signature components (Sections 06). 3. Write copy under the four voice laws (Section 05) and paste the compliance footer. If a new need arises, like a component or color that doesn't exist, add it to tokens.css and this book first, then use it. The system grows; pages never freelance.
